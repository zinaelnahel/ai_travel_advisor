# Flavors and Facades: A 3-Day Lisbon Journey

## Trip summary
This 3-day trip to Lisbon is tailored for a solo traveler with a moderate budget who is passionate about food and architecture. The itinerary balances visits to iconic architectural sites with opportunities to savor Lisbon's rich culinary scene, offering a blend of historical exploration and tasty experiences.

## Assumptions
- No specific departure city provided; flights are excluded from the budget.
- Moderate budget implies mid-range dining and accommodation.
- Mobility is assumed to be comfortable for walking and public transport.
- No special dietary restrictions mentioned.
- Trip dates are flexible; itinerary assumes mild weather typical of spring or fall.

## Day-by-day itinerary
### Day 1: Historic Lisbon and Alfama Flavors
- **Morning:** Start at Praça do Comércio, admire the Pombaline architecture, then walk to the nearby Lisbon Cathedral (Sé) and explore Alfama’s narrow streets.
- **Afternoon:** Visit the National Tile Museum (Museu Nacional do Azulejo) to see beautiful Portuguese tilework. Lunch at a traditional tasca in Alfama serving bacalhau dishes.
- **Evening:** Dinner at a Fado house in Alfama to enjoy authentic Portuguese cuisine with live Fado music.

### Day 2: Belém’s Monuments and Pastéis de Nata
- **Morning:** Take tram or taxi to Belém. Visit Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower, masterpieces of Manueline architecture.
- **Afternoon:** Lunch at Pastéis de Belém bakery to taste the original custard tarts. Visit the Monument to the Discoveries and MAAT (Museum of Art, Architecture and Technology).
- **Evening:** Return to central Lisbon for dinner at a modern Portuguese restaurant in Chiado or Bairro Alto.

### Day 3: Modern Lisbon and Mercado Foodie Experience
- **Morning:** Explore the Parque das Nações area, including the Oceanário and contemporary architecture like the Gare do Oriente station.
- **Afternoon:** Head to Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market) for a food tour sampling various Portuguese specialties from different vendors.
- **Evening:** Stroll through the Baixa district, enjoy a light dinner or drinks at a rooftop bar with views over the city.

## Practical travel tips
- Use Lisbon’s efficient tram and metro system for convenient travel; consider a 24- or 48-hour transport pass.
- Book tickets in advance for popular sites like Jerónimos Monastery and MAAT to avoid lines.
- Weather is generally mild; comfortable walking shoes are recommended due to cobblestone streets and hills.
- Moderate budget dining includes tascas, market stalls, and mid-range restaurants; avoid tourist traps in heavily trafficked areas.
- Check local opening hours as some museums close on Mondays.
- Currency: Euro (EUR).
