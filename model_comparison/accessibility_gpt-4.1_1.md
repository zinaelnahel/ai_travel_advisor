# Berlin for All: Accessible Museums & Veggie Delights

## Trip summary
Explore Berlin over 2 days, focusing on its world-class museums and vibrant vegetarian food scene. This itinerary is tailored for a wheelchair user, prioritizing step-free access and highlighting accessibility details to verify. Enjoy a comfortable, culturally rich experience within a EUR 400 budget (excluding flights).

- Trip duration: 2 full days in Berlin.
- Interests: Museums, vegetarian food.
- Accessibility: Wheelchair user, avoid stairs, highlight and verify step-free access.
- Budget: EUR 400 per person, excluding flights.

- Dates: Not specified; assumes typical weather and opening hours.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1: Museum Island & Historic Berlin
- **Morning:**  
  - Visit the Pergamon Museum (Museum Island). Features ancient artifacts and is wheelchair accessible via ramps and elevators. [Verify accessibility details before visit.]
- **Afternoon:**  
  - Lunch at Kopps, a renowned vegetarian restaurant with step-free entrance and accessible restrooms.  
  - Explore the Neues Museum (Museum Island), which is also wheelchair accessible. [Check for any temporary access changes.]
- **Evening:**  
  - Stroll (or roll) along Unter den Linden boulevard, enjoying the Brandenburg Gate (step-free plaza).  
  - Dinner at Cookies Cream, a Michelin-starred vegetarian restaurant with elevator access. [Confirm reservation and accessibility.]

### Day 2: Art, Culture & Modern Berlin
- **Morning:**  
  - Visit the Berlinische Galerie (modern art museum), which is fully wheelchair accessible. [Verify lift availability.]
- **Afternoon:**  
  - Lunch at The Bowl, a popular vegan eatery with step-free access.  
  - Explore the Jewish Museum Berlin, known for its accessible design, including ramps and elevators. [Check for any ongoing renovations.]
- **Evening:**  
  - Relax in the Tiergarten park (paved, accessible paths) or visit a local accessible café for dessert.  
  - Optional: Early evening visit to the East Side Gallery (open-air, step-free, but some uneven surfaces).

## Practical travel tips
- Berlin’s public transport (U-Bahn, S-Bahn, buses) is largely wheelchair accessible, but some older stations may lack elevators—plan routes in advance and check BVG’s accessibility info.
- Many major museums and restaurants offer step-free access and accessible restrooms, but always verify details and book in advance when possible.
- Request an accessible hotel room with roll-in shower and step-free entry.
- Carry a RADAR key (Eurokey) for accessible public toilets.
- Most vegetarian/vegan restaurants in Berlin are familiar with common dietary needs.
- Weather in Berlin is variable; check the forecast before packing and bring a rain cover for your wheelchair if needed.
