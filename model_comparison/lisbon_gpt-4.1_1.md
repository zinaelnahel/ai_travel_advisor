# Lisbon Flavors & Facades: 3 Days of Food and Architecture

## Trip summary
Experience the vibrant city of Lisbon over 3 days, exploring its renowned culinary scene and stunning architectural heritage. This itinerary is designed for a solo traveler with a moderate budget, focusing on local food experiences and the city's most iconic buildings and neighborhoods.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1: Alfama & Baixa – Old Lisbon Charm
- **Morning:** Start with a traditional Portuguese breakfast at a local pastelaria. Explore the winding streets of Alfama, visiting the Sé Cathedral and Miradouro de Santa Luzia for panoramic views.
- **Afternoon:** Walk down to Baixa, Lisbon’s downtown. Admire the grand Praça do Comércio and the elegant Rua Augusta Arch. Enjoy a lunch of bacalhau (salt cod) at a classic tasca.
- **Evening:** Dine in Alfama at a restaurant with Fado music, experiencing both the cuisine and soulful local culture.

### Day 2: Belém & Modern Lisbon
- **Morning:** Take tram 15 or a taxi to Belém. Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (UNESCO site) and the ornate Belém Tower. Try the famous Pastéis de Belém custard tarts at the original bakery.
- **Afternoon:** Explore the MAAT (Museum of Art, Architecture, and Technology) for contemporary architecture. Lunch at a riverside café.
- **Evening:** Return to central Lisbon. Sample petiscos (Portuguese tapas) in the lively Cais do Sodré district.

### Day 3: Bairro Alto & Chiado – Creative Quarters
- **Morning:** Stroll through Chiado, stopping at Café A Brasileira for coffee. Visit the Carmo Convent ruins and admire the Elevador de Santa Justa.
- **Afternoon:** Explore Bairro Alto’s street art and historic façades. Lunch at Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market), sampling dishes from top local chefs.
- **Evening:** Enjoy a sunset drink at a rooftop bar with city views. Dinner at a contemporary Portuguese restaurant in Bairro Alto.

## Practical travel tips
- Lisbon’s hills and cobblestone streets make comfortable walking shoes essential.
- Public transport (trams, metro, buses) is efficient and affordable; consider a 24-hour transport pass.
- Many restaurants close between lunch and dinner; check opening hours.
- English is widely spoken, but learning a few Portuguese phrases is appreciated.
- For moderate weather, spring (April–June) and fall (September–October) are ideal.
- Tipping is customary but modest (5–10%).
- For current weather or currency rates, check before departure.
